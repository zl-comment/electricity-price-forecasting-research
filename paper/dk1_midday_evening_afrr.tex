\begin{abstract}
This study evaluates a 4 MW/8 MWh price-taking battery in the sequential DK1
day-ahead energy and upward-aFRR markets.  We propose Gate-aware
Renewable-Alignment Stochastic Co-Optimization (GRASCO), which links
gate-conditioned market scenarios, an ECMWF-driven weather--load proxy
forecast, two-gate battery scheduling, and ex-post 15-minute settlement.  On
211 delivery days, the proposed joint market scenarios significantly improve
the Energy Score over independent quantile scenarios ($p=0.0016$), but do not
significantly outperform empirical or published block-copula comparators.
Historical paired scenarios increase realized profit over LightGBM point
dispatch by EUR 330.6/day and reduce the daily undelivered-activation share by
1.462 percentage points.  In 330 complete proxy days, the VRE-above-load proxy
is positive in 2,097 of 7,920 hours; every positive hour is also a net-export
hour, so the proxy is not measured curtailment.  WLPF identifies proxy-positive
hours with 79.19\% precision and 61.92\% recall at 07:30, rising to 80.66\%
and 66.10\% at 12:00.  On 206 matched days, GRASCO-$25$, GRASCO-$50$, and
GRASCO-$100$ monotonically increase midday charging, realized proxy overlap,
and 17:00 SOC.  Relative to GRASCO-$0$, realized profit changes by $+13.4$,
$-18.0$, and $-71.5$ EUR/day, while evening reserve and delivery remain
statistically unchanged because reserve is fixed at 07:30 and already close
to the battery power limit.  The results support probabilistic scheduling and
renewable-rich charge alignment, but not a claim of actual curtailment
reduction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Research question and contribution}

The research question is whether a price-taking battery can use information
available at the two DK1 market gates to align midday charging with
renewable-rich conditions while preserving profitable and deliverable evening
upward aFRR.  The contribution is a complete data-to-settlement experiment,
not a claim that joint energy--reserve optimization, stochastic programming,
or CVaR is itself new.  Those ingredients are established in the storage and
sequential-market literature~\cite{toubeau2021,allawati2021,mancini2024,rockafellar2000}.

The method proposed in this paper is named \emph{Gate-aware
Renewable-Alignment Stochastic Co-Optimization} (GRASCO).  GRASCO combines
two models developed for this study: \emph{Gate-Conditioned Joint Scenarios}
(GCJS) for market uncertainty and a \emph{Weather--Load Proxy Forecast}
(WLPF) for the renewable-rich signal.  The implemented renewable-alignment
experiment uses the empirically stronger pooled Gaussian-copula market
scenarios as its frozen market input; hence GRASCO denotes the complete
two-gate decision architecture and renewable-alignment mechanism, rather than
one particular copula estimator.  Its experimental variants are
GRASCO-$0$, GRASCO-$25$, GRASCO-$50$, and GRASCO-$100$, where the suffix is
the alignment credit $\rho$ in EUR/MWh.  GRASCO-$0$ is the market-only
control; the positive values are pre-specified sensitivity arms.  The full
optimization model is stated in Section~\ref{sec:grasco-method}, and WLPF is
stated in Section~\ref{sec:wlpf}.

The paper makes three distinctions throughout.  First, point forecasts,
probabilistic scenarios, and battery decisions are different objects.  Second,
methods reproduced from their original descriptions are labelled as
\emph{DK1 reconstructions} and are cited to their source papers; they are not
reported as replications of the authors' original numerical studies.  Third,
the observed VRE-above-load quantity is a reproducible diagnostic proxy, not
measured curtailment.  These distinctions determine the method taxonomy in
Table~\ref{tab:method-provenance} and the admissible claims in
Table~\ref{tab:claim-boundaries}.

\section{Scope and terminology}

This section distinguishes system-level renewable energy utilization from ex-post proxy metrics that can be evaluated using the available DK1 data and simulated battery schedules. The repository-wide terminology and evidence rules are defined in \texttt{CONVENTIONS.md}. Variable renewable energy (VRE) refers only to wind and solar generation. The DK1 data do not contain unconstrained available VRE generation, curtailment instructions, observed curtailed energy, or source tracing for battery charging. Consequently, the DK1 proxy metrics defined below must not be reported as actual curtailment reduction or as an observed renewable utilization rate.

Let $t\in\mathcal{T}$ denote a 15-minute settlement interval and let $\Delta t=0.25\,\mathrm{h}$. UTC timestamps remain the unique data keys and are mapped to Europe/Copenhagen local delivery time only when defining reporting windows. For delivery day $d$, the midday and evening-peak sets are fixed as
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{M}_d
  &=\{t: t\text{ falls between 10:00 and 15:59 on day }d\}, \\
  \mathcal{E}_d
  &=\{t: t\text{ falls between 17:00 and 21:59 on day }d\}.
\end{align}
All UTC observations are retained on daylight-saving transition days; repeated local clock times are not deduplicated.

Two audited facts motivate fixing $\mathcal M_d$ and $\mathcal E_d$ at these
clock hours.  Across 335 audited days, the day-ahead price averages
64.52~EUR/MWh in $\mathcal M_d$ against 129.10~EUR/MWh in $\mathcal E_d$, and
the upward-aFRR capacity price averages 9.84~EUR/MW against 36.86~EUR/MW; the
evening day-ahead price exceeds the midday price on 89.9\% of days, and the
evening capacity price exceeds the midday price on 93.7\% of days.  Over the
same days, the mean wind-plus-solar-to-load ratio falls from 0.800 in
$\mathcal M_d$ to 0.713 in $\mathcal E_d$, and across 331 complete
generation-and-load days the day-level correlation between this
midday-to-evening decline in the VRE-to-load ratio and the corresponding rise
in net load is 0.923 (authors' calculations from Energinet settlement
data~\cite{energineteds}).  These are audited descriptive statistics, not a
pre-registered confirmatory test; they motivate, but do not by themselves
establish, the decision results reported in Section~\ref{sec:results-main}.

\section{Data-to-settlement research chain}

The implemented analysis has three connected branches rather than one linear
sequence:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{market}}_{<d,g}
  &\longrightarrow \widehat{\Xi}^{(g)}_{d,s}
  \longrightarrow u_d
  \longrightarrow z_{d,t}
  \longrightarrow J_d,
  \label{eq:research-chain}\tag{C1}\\
  \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{weather+lag}}_{<d,g}
  &\longrightarrow \widehat X^{\mathrm{proxy},g}_{d,h}
  \longrightarrow \left\{\text{forecast scores},u_d^{(\rho)}\right\},
  \label{eq:proxy-forecast-chain}\tag{C2}\\
  \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{proxy,obs}}_{d,h}
  &\longrightarrow X^{\mathrm{proxy}}_{d,h}
  \longrightarrow J_d.
  \label{eq:proxy-diagnostic-chain}\tag{C3}
\end{align}
Here, $\mathcal{D}_{<d,g}$ contains only information visible by gate $g$.
Chain~\eqref{eq:research-chain} is the deployed market-decision chain.
Chain~\eqref{eq:proxy-forecast-chain} measures whether a gate-safe
renewable-rich signal exists and supplies it to the proxy-aware experimental
arms.  The market-only baseline is recovered at $\rho=0$, while $\rho>0$
allows the gate-visible signal to affect charge timing.  Chain~\eqref{eq:proxy-diagnostic-chain}
uses realized wind, solar, and load only after delivery; the realized proxy is
never supplied to either decision gate.

The forecasting target vector for scenario $s$ is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\Xi}^{(g)}_{d,s}
  =\left\{
  \widehat{\lambda}^{\mathrm{DA},(g)}_{d,s,t},
  \widehat{\kappa}^{\mathrm{up},(g)}_{d,s,h},
  \widehat{a}^{(g)}_{d,s,t},
  \widehat{\lambda}^{\mathrm{act},(g)}_{d,s,t},
  \widehat{\lambda}^{\mathrm{imb},(g)}_{d,s,t}
  \right\},
  \label{eq:forecast-vector}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda^{\mathrm{DA}}$ is the day-ahead energy price,
$\kappa^{\mathrm{up}}$ is the upward-aFRR capacity price, $a$ is the
activation-energy coefficient defined by Eq.~\eqref{eq:activation-coefficient},
$\lambda^{\mathrm{act}}$ is the activation
price, and $\lambda^{\mathrm{imb}}$ is the imbalance price used for shortfall
and recovery settlement.  The experiment uses 50 equal-weight scenarios.  The
market vector in Eq.~\eqref{eq:forecast-vector} is read from the frozen S3
scenario archives, whose construction is stated in
Section~\ref{sec:market-forecast-method}; it is not generated by the
weather--load regression in
Chain~\eqref{eq:proxy-forecast-chain}.  The former drives the optimizer, while
the latter forecasts only the diagnostic proxy.
The
07:30 solve chooses the hourly reserve vector.  At 12:00 the realized capacity
price and updated energy-side information are introduced, while the 07:30
reserve commitment remains frozen.  The 12:00 programme first maximizes
expected activation delivery and then maximizes the risk-adjusted profit within
a $10^{-9}$ MWh delivery tolerance.

The observed DK1 inputs and their native resolutions are
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{market}}
  &=\{\lambda^{\mathrm{DA}}_t,
      \kappa^{\mathrm{up}}_h,
      Q^{\mathrm{proc}}_h,
      A^{\mathrm{up}}_t,
      \lambda^{\mathrm{act}}_t,
      \lambda^{\mathrm{imb}}_t\},
  \label{eq:market-data}\\
  \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{proxy}}
  &=\{P^{\mathrm{wind,obs}}_h,
      P^{\mathrm{solar,obs}}_h,
      L^{\mathrm{obs}}_h,
      F_{b,h},C_{k,h},H_h\}.
  \label{eq:proxy-data}
\end{align}
Here, $F_{b,h}$ is the signed exchange on border $b$ (positive for imports),
$C_{k,h}$ is generation in non-VRE category $k$, and $H_h$ is observed
Power-to-Heat consumption.  $Q^{\mathrm{proc}}_h$ supplies the upper bound on
the battery's reserve commitment, while $A^{\mathrm{up}}_t$ and
$Q^{\mathrm{proc}}_{h(t)}$ produce the realized coefficient in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:activation-coefficient}.  Gate-specific ECMWF weather runs are introduced
in Section~\ref{sec:wlpf} below as forecast inputs rather than as ex-post observations.
Energy, activation, and imbalance settlement use 15-minute intervals; aFRR
capacity and the proxy inputs use hourly observations.  Hourly quantities are
mapped to their four settlement intervals without changing the UTC key.
Equations~\eqref{eq:market-data} and~\eqref{eq:forecast-vector} therefore feed
the optimization branch, whereas Eq.~\eqref{eq:proxy-data} feeds the ex-post
proxy branch.

\section{System-level renewable energy utilization}

For VRE technology $v\in\mathcal{V}=\{\mathrm{wind},\mathrm{solar}\}$, let $P^{\mathrm{avail}}_{v,t}$ denote the power that would be available without curtailment, and let $P^{\mathrm{used}}_{v,t}$ denote the power accepted by the system after dispatch. The actual curtailed power is
\begin{equation}
  P^{\mathrm{curt}}_{v,t}
  =
  \left[P^{\mathrm{avail}}_{v,t}-P^{\mathrm{used}}_{v,t}\right]^+,
  \qquad [x]^+=\max(x,0).
  \label{eq:true-curtailment-power}
\end{equation}
For an evaluation horizon $\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{T}$, curtailed energy, the curtailment rate, and the renewable utilization rate are
\begin{align}
  E^{\mathrm{curt}}_{\mathcal{H}}
  &=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{H}}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}
  P^{\mathrm{curt}}_{v,t}\Delta t,
  \label{eq:true-curtailment-energy}\\
  CR_{\mathcal{H}}
  &=\frac{E^{\mathrm{curt}}_{\mathcal{H}}}
  {\displaystyle\sum_{t\in\mathcal{H}}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}
  P^{\mathrm{avail}}_{v,t}\Delta t},
  \label{eq:true-curtailment-rate}\\
  UR_{\mathcal{H}}&=1-CR_{\mathcal{H}}.
  \label{eq:true-utilization-rate}
\end{align}
Equations~\eqref{eq:true-curtailment-power}--\eqref{eq:true-utilization-rate} require available and utilized generation to share the same technology, location, and temporal boundaries. Realized wind and solar generation already reflect dispatch, network constraints, and possible curtailment; they therefore cannot substitute for $P^{\mathrm{avail}}_{v,t}$.

The contribution of battery storage to curtailment reduction requires a controlled system-level counterfactual. Let scenario 0 exclude the battery and scenario 1 include it, while holding demand, weather, available generation, network constraints, and all other generating-unit constraints fixed. The model-based curtailment reduction attributable to the battery is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta E^{\mathrm{curt}}_{\mathrm{BESS},\mathcal{H}}
  =E^{\mathrm{curt},0}_{\mathcal{H}}-E^{\mathrm{curt},1}_{\mathcal{H}}.
  \label{eq:counterfactual-curtailment-reduction}
\end{equation}
A positive value in Eq.~\eqref{eq:counterfactual-curtailment-reduction} can be reported as model-based curtailment reduction. A claim that the battery is charged from VRE additionally requires source tracing or an explicit coupling constraint, for example
\begin{equation}
  0\leq P^{\mathrm{ch,VRE}}_t
  \leq\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}P^{\mathrm{used}}_{v,t},
  \qquad P^{\mathrm{ch,VRE}}_t\leq P^{\mathrm{ch}}_t.
  \label{eq:source-tracing}
\end{equation}
The current price-taking DK1 simulation does not provide the source tracing required by Eq.~\eqref{eq:source-tracing}; hence, $P^{\mathrm{ch,VRE}}_t$ is not evaluated.

\section{Ex-post proxy metrics observable in DK1}

Observed VRE power, the VRE-to-load ratio, the local residual-load proxy, and the local VRE-above-load proxy are defined as
\begin{align}
  P^{\mathrm{VRE,obs}}_t
  &=P^{\mathrm{wind,obs}}_t+P^{\mathrm{solar,obs}}_t,
  \label{eq:observed-vre}\\
  \rho^{\mathrm{VRE}}_t
  &=\frac{P^{\mathrm{VRE,obs}}_t}{L^{\mathrm{obs}}_t},
  \qquad L^{\mathrm{obs}}_t>0,
  \label{eq:vre-share}\\
  NL^{\mathrm{proxy}}_t
  &=L^{\mathrm{obs}}_t-P^{\mathrm{VRE,obs}}_t,
  \label{eq:net-load-proxy}\\
  X^{\mathrm{proxy}}_t
  &=\left[-NL^{\mathrm{proxy}}_t\right]^+
   =\left[P^{\mathrm{VRE,obs}}_t-L^{\mathrm{obs}}_t\right]^+.
  \label{eq:surplus-proxy}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:observed-vre} first combines the two observed VRE
technologies.  Its output is divided by load in Eq.~\eqref{eq:vre-share} and
subtracted from load in Eq.~\eqref{eq:net-load-proxy}.  Taking the positive
part of the negative residual load then produces
$X^{\mathrm{proxy}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:surplus-proxy}.  This output is reused in
the balance decomposition, the charging-overlap metrics, the forecast target,
and the positive-hour classification; none of those downstream calculations
changes its definition.
Neither $NL^{\mathrm{proxy}}_t$ nor $X^{\mathrm{proxy}}_t$ accounts for conventional generation, cross-border exchanges, network constraints, or actual curtailment. They must therefore be described as a local residual-load proxy and a local VRE-above-load proxy, respectively, rather than as system net load or actual renewable surplus.

\subsection{Exchange, conventional-generation, and Power-to-Heat decomposition}

The signed net exchange, gross export, gross import, and observed non-VRE
generation are
\begin{align}
  F_t&=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}F_{b,t},
  &E_t&=[-F_t]^+,
  &I_t&=[F_t]^+,
  \label{eq:exchange-components}\\
  C_t&=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}C_{k,t},
  &\mathcal{K}&=\{\text{central, local, commercial, local self-consumption,}
  \nonumber\\[-0.4em]
  &&&\hspace{5.4em}\text{hydro, unknown}\}.
  \label{eq:conventional-components}
\end{align}
Equations~\eqref{eq:exchange-components} and
\eqref{eq:conventional-components} aggregate the border and generation-category
inputs in Eq.~\eqref{eq:proxy-data}; their outputs enter the measured balance
below.
The Energinet settlement table obeys the hourly balance
\begin{equation}
  P^{\mathrm{VRE,obs}}_t+C_t+F_t
  =L^{\mathrm{obs}}_t+\varepsilon_t,
  \label{eq:observed-balance}
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_t$ is the numerical balance residual.  Substituting
Eq.~\eqref{eq:observed-balance} into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:surplus-proxy} proceeds through
\begin{equation}
  P^{\mathrm{VRE,obs}}_t-L^{\mathrm{obs}}_t
  =-C_t-F_t+\varepsilon_t
  =E_t-I_t-C_t+\varepsilon_t,
  \label{eq:proxy-balance-substitution}
\end{equation}
because $F_t=I_t-E_t$.  Applying the same positive-part operator used in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:surplus-proxy} gives
\begin{equation}
  X^{\mathrm{proxy}}_t
  =\left[E_t-I_t-C_t+\varepsilon_t\right]^+.
  \label{eq:proxy-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Thus a proxy-positive hour may be an exporting hour rather than an hour in
which energy lacks a system use.  The export coverage and the conventional
share of export over a set $\mathcal{H}$ are reported without adding their
numerators:
\begin{align}
  EC_{\mathcal{H}}
  &=\frac{\sum_{t\in\mathcal{H}}
  \min(X^{\mathrm{proxy}}_t,E_t)}
  {\sum_{t\in\mathcal{H}}X^{\mathrm{proxy}}_t},
  \label{eq:export-coverage}\\
  CS_{\mathcal{H}}
  &=\frac{\sum_{t\in\mathcal{H}}C_t}
  {\sum_{t\in\mathcal{H}}E_t}.
  \label{eq:conventional-export-share}
\end{align}

Power-to-Heat is already contained in $L^{\mathrm{obs}}_t$.  It is therefore
not subtracted again in Eq.~\eqref{eq:proxy-decomposition}.  Its observed
absorption is evaluated through the explicit no-Power-to-Heat counterfactual
\begin{align}
  X^{\mathrm{noP2H}}_t
  &=\left[P^{\mathrm{VRE,obs}}_t-
  (L^{\mathrm{obs}}_t-H_t)\right]^+,
  \label{eq:no-p2h-proxy}\\
  A^{\mathrm{P2H}}_t
  &=X^{\mathrm{noP2H}}_t-X^{\mathrm{proxy}}_t,
  \label{eq:p2h-absorption}\\
  HS_{\mathcal{H}}
  &=\frac{\sum_{t\in\mathcal{H}}A^{\mathrm{P2H}}_t}
  {\sum_{t\in\mathcal{H}}X^{\mathrm{noP2H}}_t}.
  \label{eq:p2h-share}
\end{align}
$A^{\mathrm{P2H}}_t$ is an observed accounting counterfactual, not an estimate
of curtailment avoided by electric heating.  The dependency is
$H_t\rightarrow(L^{\mathrm{obs}}_t-H_t)\rightarrow
X^{\mathrm{noP2H}}_t\rightarrow A^{\mathrm{P2H}}_t\rightarrow HS_{\mathcal H}$;
$H_t$ is not an additional subtraction from Eq.~\eqref{eq:proxy-decomposition}.

The simulated midday charging energy on delivery day $d$ is
\begin{equation}
  E^{\mathrm{mid}}_{\mathrm{ch},d}
  =\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}_d}P^{\mathrm{ch}}_t\Delta t.
  \label{eq:midday-charge}
\end{equation}
The temporal overlap between simulated charging and the local VRE-above-load proxy is
\begin{equation}
  E^{\mathrm{overlap}}_{\mathrm{ch},d}
  =\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}_d}
  \min\!\left(P^{\mathrm{ch}}_t,X^{\mathrm{proxy}}_t\right)\Delta t.
  \label{eq:overlap-charge}
\end{equation}
Two proxy ratios with distinct denominators are then reported:
\begin{align}
  AS^{\mathrm{ch}}_d
  &=\frac{E^{\mathrm{overlap}}_{\mathrm{ch},d}}
  {E^{\mathrm{mid}}_{\mathrm{ch},d}},
  \qquad E^{\mathrm{mid}}_{\mathrm{ch},d}>0,
  \label{eq:alignment-share}\\
  AR^{\mathrm{proxy}}_d
  &=\frac{E^{\mathrm{overlap}}_{\mathrm{ch},d}}
  {\displaystyle\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}_d}X^{\mathrm{proxy}}_t\Delta t},
  \qquad \sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}_d}X^{\mathrm{proxy}}_t\Delta t>0.
  \label{eq:proxy-absorption-ratio}
\end{align}
$AS^{\mathrm{ch}}_d$ is the share of simulated midday charging that coincides with the local VRE-above-load proxy. $AR^{\mathrm{proxy}}_d$ is the share of that proxy whose power magnitude temporally overlaps with simulated charging. Both are temporal alignment metrics. Neither traces the physical origin of electricity nor equals the renewable utilization rate in Eq.~\eqref{eq:true-utilization-rate}.
Both ratios inherit the same numerator from Eq.~\eqref{eq:overlap-charge}; only
their denominators differ.  The numerator couples the deployed charge from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:decision-vector} with the ex-post proxy from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:surplus-proxy}, so it cannot be computed before both the decision
and realized-observation branches are available.

To avoid relying exclusively on the strict condition $P^{\mathrm{VRE,obs}}_t>L^{\mathrm{obs}}_t$, a renewable-rich indicator may be defined using a threshold $\tau$. The threshold must be fixed using the training period or an independent reference period and must not be selected from test outcomes:
\begin{align}
  I^{\mathrm{rich}}_t
  &=\mathbb{I}\!\left(\rho^{\mathrm{VRE}}_t\geq\tau\right),
  \label{eq:rich-indicator}\\
  E^{\mathrm{rich}}_{\mathrm{ch},d}
  &=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}_d}
  I^{\mathrm{rich}}_tP^{\mathrm{ch}}_t\Delta t.
  \label{eq:rich-charge}
\end{align}
Until $\tau$ is frozen on an independent sample, Eq.~\eqref{eq:rich-charge} is a sensitivity metric rather than a confirmatory primary outcome.

Charging during negative day-ahead prices is defined as
\begin{equation}
  E^{\mathrm{neg}}_{\mathrm{ch},d}
  =\sum_{t\in\mathcal{M}_d}
  \mathbb{I}\!\left(\lambda^{\mathrm{DA}}_t<0\right)
  P^{\mathrm{ch}}_t\Delta t.
  \label{eq:negative-price-charge}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:negative-price-charge} measures an economic response to negative prices. Negative prices can indicate insufficient system flexibility, but they do not independently establish that wind or solar generation was curtailed.

\section{Market forecasting and scenario construction}
\label{sec:market-forecast-method}

The market-forecast branch is evaluated separately from the WLPF branch.  It
produces the four-target vector
$\widehat\Xi^{(g)}_{d,s}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:forecast-vector}.  The point
baselines are previous-day persistence (PERS)~\cite{murphy1992}, the Lasso-estimated
autoregressive model (LEAR)~\cite{zielweron2018}, and LightGBM
(LGBM)~\cite{ke2017lightgbm}.  These methods are established comparators, not
contributions of this paper.  The frozen LGBM arm supplies point predictions
for all four targets; the separately trained LEAR and LGBM reference arms are
available for day-ahead and capacity prices.

All probabilistic models use a rolling origin and the same chronological
information blocks.  For delivery day $d$, the marginal fit window is the 84
calendar days $d-119$ through $d-36$, probability calibration uses $d-35$
through $d-8$, and $d-7$ through $d-1$ is a clearing gap.  No missing target
or feature is filled.  Quantile levels are
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal A=\{0.005,0.01,0.025,0.05,0.1,0.2,\ldots,0.8,
  0.9,0.95,0.975,0.99,0.995\}.
  \label{eq:quantile-grid}
\end{equation}
For day-ahead price, capacity price, and activation price, conditional
quantiles are estimated by linear quantile regression~\cite{koenker1978}.
Day-ahead and capacity models are hour specific; activation price is pooled
over the 24 hours with six four-hour period indicators.  Predictors contain
only gate-visible lagged values, seven-day visible means, and calendar terms.
The activation-volume marginal is a hurdle model: a logistic regression
estimates the zero-event probability and quantile regression on log positive
volumes estimates the conditional positive distribution.  Estimated
quantiles are sorted to remove crossing and linearly interpolated when a
scenario requests a probability between grid points.

GCJS is the joint scenario model proposed and implemented in this paper.  Let
$u_{d,j}$ be the randomized probability integral transform (PIT) of dimension
$j$, where $j$ indexes the $4\times24=96$ target--hour cells, and let
\begin{equation}
  z_{d,j}=\Phi^{-1}\!\left(
  \min\{0.999,\max(0.001,u_{d,j})\}
  \right).
  \label{eq:normal-score}
\end{equation}
Pairwise-complete correlations of $z_{d,j}$ form $R_d$.  A principal-axis
factor model gives the regularized Gaussian-copula correlation
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_d=B_dB_d^{\top}+\Psi_d,
  \qquad \operatorname{diag}(\Sigma_d)=\mathbf 1,
  \label{eq:gcjs-correlation}
\end{equation}
where $\Psi_d$ is diagonal.  Ranks one, two, and three are compared only on
the probability-calibration block, and the rank with the lowest standardized
joint Energy Score is selected.  Drawing
$Z_s\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_d)$, applying $\Phi$ componentwise, and mapping
the resulting uniforms through the marginal inverse quantiles yields 100
forecast scenarios; 50 equal-weight scenarios are retained for optimization.
This is a Gaussian-copula construction in the standard sense of
Nelsen~\cite{nelsen2006}, while the rolling marginals, gate-safe information
sets, low-rank selection, and 96-dimensional DK1 target organization are the
present implementation.

The scenario comparisons isolate distinct modeling choices.  Rolling
climatology and persistence are classical reference forecasts~\cite{murphy1992}.
Independent
quantile regression (QR-Ind) samples every marginal independently.  Historical
paired scenarios (Hist-P) resample one complete historical day for all
targets, whereas historical independent scenarios (Hist-I) resample a day
separately for each target; these are present implementations of empirical
sample-based scenarios~\cite{shapiro2014}.  The empirical-copula comparator (EC) resamples
complete PIT-rank rows.  PGC-NoCross sets cross-target correlations to zero
while retaining within-target time dependence; PGC-NoTime sets cross-hour
correlations to zero while retaining same-hour cross-target dependence.  These
two arms and QR-Ind are the formal dependence ablations.  The sequential
block-copula comparator implements the multi-phase dependence structure of
Al-Lawati et al.~\cite{allawati2021} under the common DK1 marginals.  The
weather-augmented arm (WX-PGC) is an explicit information sensitivity using
published wind and solar forecasts; it is not part of GCJS and is evaluated
on the 209 days for which those inputs are complete.

At the 12:00 gate, the already published capacity-price path may condition the
remaining 72 market dimensions.  GCJS uses the Gaussian conditional mean and
Schur-complement covariance; EC uses the ten nearest historical capacity
paths.  These are information-update experiments, not separate proposed
methods.  Their empirical performance is reported before any decision result,
because a later gate does not by itself guarantee a better forecast.

Point errors use MAE and RMSE.  Marginal probabilistic performance uses the
pinball loss, sample CRPS, PIT distance, interval coverage, and the activation
zero-event Brier score.  Joint scenarios use the Energy Score and Variogram
Score~\cite{gneitingraftery2007}.  Daily loss differences are tested with the
Diebold--Mariano statistic~\cite{dieboldmariano1995} and six-lag
Newey--West covariance~\cite{neweywest1987}; uncertainty intervals use a
seven-day moving-block bootstrap~\cite{kunsch1989} with 2,000 replications.
All forecast scores are negatively oriented: lower is better.

\section{Gate-available weather, load, and proxy forecasts: WLPF}
\label{sec:wlpf}

WLPF is proposed in this paper as the gate-safe renewable-alignment signal for
GRASCO.  It is distinct from WX-PGC: WX-PGC augments the market marginals,
whereas WLPF predicts the ex-post VRE-above-load proxy in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:surplus-proxy}.

For each delivery day $d$, archived ECMWF IFS forecasts are selected by model
initialization time rather than by the data-download time.  Let $r(d,g)$ denote
the selected run and impose a conservative six-hour production delay:
\begin{align}
  r(d,07{:}30)&=(d-2)\;18{:}00\ \mathrm{UTC},
  &a(d,07{:}30)&=r(d,07{:}30)+6\ \mathrm{h},
  \label{eq:weather-run-0730}\\
  r(d,12{:}00)&=(d-1)\;00{:}00\ \mathrm{UTC},
  &a(d,12{:}00)&=r(d,12{:}00)+6\ \mathrm{h}.
  \label{eq:weather-run-1200}
\end{align}
The implementation asserts $a(d,g)\leq G(d,g)$, where $G(d,g)$ is the UTC
instant corresponding to the civil-time gate on day $d-1$.  The weather vector
$w^{(g)}_{d,h}$ contains the equal-grid DK1 means of 2-m temperature, 100-m
wind speed, shortwave radiation, and total cloud cover, together with the
spatial standard deviation of 100-m wind speed, from run $r(d,g)$.  The run
archive and its distinction between initialization time and public availability
are documented by Open-Meteo~\cite{openmeteo2026singleruns}.  The underlying
numerical weather prediction system is ECMWF IFS HRES~\cite{ecmwfifs}; the
retrieved fields are archived model forecasts rather than weather observations.

Weather fields are sampled on a $3\times3$ grid defined by latitudes
$\{55.5,56.25,57.0\}^{\circ}\mathrm{N}$ and longitudes
$\{8.0,9.0,10.0\}^{\circ}\mathrm{E}$, yielding nine points over Jutland
and adjacent waters.  For each valid hour and model run, the weather
variables are aggregated using an unweighted arithmetic mean across the nine
points.  For a weather field $x$ and grid index $j$, the aggregation is
\begin{align}
  \overline{x}^{(g)}_{d,h}
  &=\frac{1}{9}\sum_{j=1}^{9}x^{(g)}_{d,h,j},
  \label{eq:weather-grid-mean}\\
  \sigma^{(g)}_{U,d,h}
  &=\left[\frac{1}{9}\sum_{j=1}^{9}
  \left(U^{(g)}_{d,h,j}-\overline U^{(g)}_{d,h}\right)^2\right]^{1/2}.
  \label{eq:weather-wind-spread}
\end{align}
The weather vector is therefore
\begin{equation}
  w^{(g)}_{d,h}
  =\left[
  \overline T^{(g)}_{d,h},\overline U^{(g)}_{d,h},
  \sigma^{(g)}_{U,d,h},\overline R^{(g)}_{d,h},
  \overline C^{(g)}_{d,h}
  \right]^{\top},
  \label{eq:weather-vector}
\end{equation}
where $T,U,R,C$ denote 2-m temperature, 100-m wind speed, shortwave
radiation, and total cloud cover.  No bidding-zone boundary mask, land--sea mask, area weighting, or
installed-capacity weighting is applied.  These fields should therefore be
interpreted as regional DK1 weather proxies rather than exact spatial averages
over the DK1 bidding zone.

Separate component models are fitted for
$q\in\{\mathrm{wind},\mathrm{solar},\mathrm{load}\}$.  The design vector is
\begin{equation}
  \phi^{(g)}_{q,d,h}
  =\left[1,\ \operatorname{Fourier}_4(h),\
  \operatorname{DoW}(d),\ w^{(g)}_{d,h},\
  T_{d,h}^{2},U_{d,h}^{2},U_{d,h}^{3},\
  q_{d-7,h}^{\mathrm{obs}},q_{d-14,h}^{\mathrm{obs}}\right]^{\!\top},
  \label{eq:proxy-feature-vector}
\end{equation}
where $T$ and $U$ are temperature and 100-m wind speed, respectively;
$\operatorname{Fourier}_4(h)$ contains sine and cosine terms for harmonics
one through four; and $\operatorname{DoW}(d)$ contains six weekday indicators
with Monday omitted.  Ordinary least squares is fitted only on the existing
84-calendar-day training window from $d-119$ through $d-36$.  Define
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal F_d
  =\{(d',h):d-119\leq d'\leq d-36
  \text{ and every required input and target is observed}\}.
  \label{eq:proxy-training-set}
\end{equation}
The fitted coefficients and nonnegative component forecast are then
\begin{align}
  \widehat\beta^{(g)}_{q,d}
  &=\arg\min_{\beta}\sum_{(d',h)\in\mathcal{F}_d}
  \left(q^{\mathrm{obs}}_{d',h}
  -\phi^{(g)\top}_{q,d',h}\beta\right)^2,
  \label{eq:component-ols}\\
  \widehat q^{(g)}_{d,h}
  &=\left[\phi^{(g)\top}_{q,d,h}
  \widehat\beta^{(g)}_{q,d}\right]^+.
  \label{eq:component-forecast}
\end{align}
The continuous dependency is
$r(d,g)\rightarrow w^{(g)}_{d,h}\rightarrow
\phi^{(g)}_{q,d,h}\rightarrow\widehat\beta^{(g)}_{q,d}\rightarrow
\widehat q^{(g)}_{d,h}$.  No target from delivery day $d$ enters
$\mathcal{F}_d$.  Missing inputs remain
missing and the affected forecast is not evaluated.  The observed 7- and
14-day lags are assigned the same 75.75-hour publication delay used by the DK1
data audit, and the implementation separately verifies their availability at
each gate.  The gate-available proxy
forecast is then
\begin{equation}
  \widehat X^{\mathrm{proxy},g}_{d,h}
  =\left[\widehat P^{\mathrm{wind},g}_{d,h}
  +\widehat P^{\mathrm{solar},g}_{d,h}
  -\widehat L^{g}_{d,h}\right]^+.
  \label{eq:gate-proxy-forecast}
\end{equation}
Thus Eqs.~\eqref{eq:proxy-training-set}--\eqref{eq:component-forecast} produce
three component forecasts, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate-proxy-forecast} combines
their outputs using the same positive-part operation as the realized target in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:surplus-proxy}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:gate-proxy-forecast} is a model-generated forecast of the
same ex-post proxy in Eq.~\eqref{eq:surplus-proxy}; it is neither an official
TSO load forecast nor a forecast of physical curtailment.  It is evaluated as
a forecast target and supplied only to the explicitly labelled proxy-aware
arms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:proxy-aware-objective}; the market-only arms retain the
original information set and provide the control comparison.

For every gate and evaluation set $\mathcal{Q}$, the implementation reports
component MAE, proxy MAE and RMSE, and positive-hour precision and recall:
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{MAE}^{g}_{q}
  &=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|}\sum_{(d,h)\in\mathcal{Q}}
  \left|\widehat q^{g}_{d,h}-q^{\mathrm{obs}}_{d,h}\right|,
  \label{eq:component-mae}\\
  \operatorname{MAE}^{g}_{X}
  &=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|}\sum_{(d,h)\in\mathcal{Q}}
  \left|\widehat X^{\mathrm{proxy},g}_{d,h}
  -X^{\mathrm{proxy}}_{d,h}\right|,
  \label{eq:proxy-mae}\\
  \operatorname{RMSE}^{g}_{X}
  &=\sqrt{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|}\sum_{(d,h)\in\mathcal{Q}}
  \left(\widehat X^{\mathrm{proxy},g}_{d,h}
  -X^{\mathrm{proxy}}_{d,h}\right)^2},
  \label{eq:proxy-rmse}\\
  \operatorname{Precision}^{g}_{X}
  &=\frac{\sum_{(d,h)\in\mathcal Q}\widehat I^{g}_{d,h}I_{d,h}}
  {\sum_{(d,h)\in\mathcal Q}\widehat I^{g}_{d,h}},
  &\operatorname{Recall}^{g}_{X}
  &=\frac{\sum_{(d,h)\in\mathcal Q}\widehat I^{g}_{d,h}I_{d,h}}
  {\sum_{(d,h)\in\mathcal Q}I_{d,h}},
  \label{eq:proxy-classification}
\end{align}
where $I_{d,h}=\mathbb{I}(X^{\mathrm{proxy}}_{d,h}>0)$ and
$\widehat I^{g}_{d,h}=\mathbb{I}(\widehat X^{\mathrm{proxy},g}_{d,h}>0)$.
Equations~\eqref{eq:component-mae}--\eqref{eq:proxy-classification} compare
the forecast branch with its realized counterpart; they do not feed any
decision variable.
The information value between the gates is summarized by
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{MAR}_{07{:}30\rightarrow12{:}00}
  =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|}\sum_{(d,h)\in\mathcal{Q}}
  \left|\widehat X^{\mathrm{proxy},12{:}00}_{d,h}
  -\widehat X^{\mathrm{proxy},07{:}30}_{d,h}\right|.
  \label{eq:gate-revision}
\end{equation}

\section{Proposed GRASCO decision model}
\label{sec:grasco-method}

GRASCO is proposed in this paper.  Its two-gate nonanticipativity follows the
actual DK1 information sequence, while its stochastic-programming ingredients
are related to established chance-constrained, sequential, and three-stage
storage formulations~\cite{toubeau2021,allawati2021,mancini2024}.  The new
element evaluated here is the explicit coupling of a gate-safe renewable-rich
forecast to the two-gate energy--reserve schedule and its subsequent
15-minute realized settlement trace.

The common battery has rated power $\bar P=4$ MW, energy capacity
$\bar S=8$ MWh, round-trip efficiency $\eta^{\mathrm{rt}}=0.85$, one-way
efficiencies $\eta_c=\eta_d=\sqrt{0.85}$, and initial and terminal SOC
$S_0=S_T=4.4$ MWh.  The market-share parameter is
$\alpha^{\mathrm{share}}=1$ for the main arm.  The programme contains three
variable layers shown in Table~\ref{tab:decision-layers}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \small
  \caption{Decision layers and their information dependence.}
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
    \toprule
    Layer & Variables & Dependence \\
    \midrule
    Common reserve & $r^{\mathrm{up}}_{d,h}$ & identical in every scenario \\
    Cluster energy plan
      & $P^{\mathrm{ch}}_{d,c,t},P^{\mathrm{dis}}_{d,c,t},S^{\mathrm{plan}}_{d,c,t}$
      & only on $c=c(s)$ \\
    Scenario recourse
      & $P^{\mathrm{rec}}_{d,s,t},e^{\mathrm{del}}_{d,s,t},S^{\mathrm{real}}_{d,s,t}$
      & full scenario $s$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \label{tab:decision-layers}
\end{table}

At 07:30, the capacity-price paths are standardized by their historical
hour-specific standard deviations and assigned to clusters $c(s)$.  Energy
variables share one value within a cluster, which is the implemented
nonanticipativity rule.  These 07:30 cluster energy plans value the common
reserve decision but are not deployed.  At 12:00 the realized capacity-price
path is inserted, all scenario labels are set to one cluster, the 07:30 reserve
vector is fixed, and the resulting unique energy plan is deployed:
\begin{equation}
  u_d=\left\{
  r^{\mathrm{up}}_{d,h},
  P^{\mathrm{ch}}_{d,t},P^{\mathrm{dis}}_{d,t},
  S^{\mathrm{plan}}_{d,t}
  \right\}.
  \label{eq:decision-vector}
\end{equation}
The complete stochastic-programme vector, including variables used only inside
scenario evaluation, is
\begin{equation}
  x_d=\left\{
  r^{\mathrm{up}}_{d,h},
  P^{\mathrm{ch}}_{d,c,t},P^{\mathrm{dis}}_{d,c,t},S^{\mathrm{plan}}_{d,c,t},
  P^{\mathrm{rec}}_{d,s,t},e^{\mathrm{del}}_{d,s,t},S^{\mathrm{real}}_{d,s,t},
  q^{g}_{d,c,h}
  \right\}.
  \label{eq:programme-vector}
\end{equation}
Thus $u_d\subset x_d$: $x_d$ is optimized, whereas $u_d$ is the frozen plan
passed to ex-post settlement.

The procured market volume and battery rating bound the common reserve before
any SOC equation is evaluated:
\begin{equation}
  0\leq r^{\mathrm{up}}_{d,h}
  \leq \min\!\left(
  \bar P,\alpha^{\mathrm{share}}Q^{\mathrm{proc}}_{d,h}
  \right).
  \label{eq:reserve-cap}
\end{equation}
Thus $Q^{\mathrm{proc}}$ is an upstream feasibility input, not a revenue or
activation variable.

For each capacity-price cluster, the pre-activation plan starts from $S_0$ and
follows
\begin{equation}
  S^{\mathrm{plan}}_{d,c,t}
  =S^{\mathrm{plan}}_{d,c,t-1}
  +\eta_c\Delta t P^{\mathrm{ch}}_{d,c,t}
  -\frac{\Delta t}{\eta_d}P^{\mathrm{dis}}_{d,c,t},
  \qquad S^{\mathrm{plan}}_{d,c,0}=S_0.
  \label{eq:planned-soc}
\end{equation}
Its state, terminal, power, and one-hour reserve-energy constraints are
\begin{align}
  0&\leq S^{\mathrm{plan}}_{d,c,t}\leq\bar S,
  &S^{\mathrm{plan}}_{d,c,T}&=S_0,
  \label{eq:planned-soc-band}\\
  P^{\mathrm{ch}}_{d,c,t}+P^{\mathrm{dis}}_{d,c,t}
  +r^{\mathrm{up}}_{d,h(t)}&\leq\bar P,
  \label{eq:planned-power}\\
  S^{\mathrm{plan}}_{d,c,t}
  &\geq\frac{T^{\mathrm{res}}}{\eta_d}r^{\mathrm{up}}_{d,h(t)},
  &T^{\mathrm{res}}&=1\ \mathrm{h}.
  \label{eq:reserve-energy}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:planned-soc} supplies the state used by
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:planned-soc-band}--\eqref{eq:reserve-energy}; the reserve cannot
be checked without first propagating the charge and discharge plan.

For scenario $s$, the cluster plan $c(s)$ and the activation coefficient from
the market forecast vector produce requested activation energy:
\begin{equation}
  e^{\mathrm{req}}_{d,s,t}
  =r^{\mathrm{up}}_{d,h(t)}a_{d,s,t}.
  \label{eq:required-activation}
\end{equation}
Delivered activation and paid recovery then modify the planned trajectory.  The
scenario state is
\begin{align}
  S^{\mathrm{real}}_{d,s,t}
  ={}&S^{\mathrm{real}}_{d,s,t-1}
    +\eta_c\Delta t\left(P^{\mathrm{ch}}_{d,c(s),t}
      +P^{\mathrm{rec}}_{d,s,t}\right)
    -\frac{\Delta t}{\eta_d}P^{\mathrm{dis}}_{d,c(s),t}
    -\frac{e^{\mathrm{del}}_{d,s,t}}{\eta_d},
  \nonumber\\
  &S^{\mathrm{real}}_{d,s,0}=S_0.
  \label{eq:realized-soc}
\end{align}
The recourse variables are coupled by
\begin{align}
  0&\leq S^{\mathrm{real}}_{d,s,t}\leq\bar S,
  &S^{\mathrm{real}}_{d,s,T}&=S_0,
  \label{eq:realized-soc-band}\\
  P^{\mathrm{ch}}_{d,c(s),t}+P^{\mathrm{dis}}_{d,c(s),t}
  +P^{\mathrm{rec}}_{d,s,t}
  +\frac{e^{\mathrm{del}}_{d,s,t}}{\Delta t}
  &\leq\bar P,
  \label{eq:realized-power}\\
  S^{\mathrm{real}}_{d,s,t}
  +\frac{e^{\mathrm{del}}_{d,s,t}}{\eta_d}
  &\geq\frac{T^{\mathrm{res}}}{\eta_d}
  r^{\mathrm{up}}_{d,h(t)},
  \label{eq:realized-reserve-energy}\\
  0\leq e^{\mathrm{del}}_{d,s,t}
  &\leq e^{\mathrm{req}}_{d,s,t}.
  \label{eq:delivery-bound}
\end{align}
The configured 07:30 ``full'' delivery mode replaces
Eq.~\eqref{eq:delivery-bound} by
$e^{\mathrm{del}}_{d,s,t}=e^{\mathrm{req}}_{d,s,t}$ inside the early reserve
valuation.  The 12:00 ``delivery-first'' mode retains the inequality and uses
the lexicographic solve below.
Equation~\eqref{eq:realized-power} tests power headroom after the energy plan,
recovery, and activation are combined.  Equation~\eqref{eq:realized-reserve-energy}
adds the current delivered energy back to the post-interval state and checks
that the pre-delivery state covered the one-hour reserve obligation.  These two
constraints separate power-driven shortfall from energy-driven shortfall.

The scenario settlement profit is
\begin{align}
  \Pi_{d,s}(x_d)
  ={}&\sum_t\Delta t\lambda^{\mathrm{DA}}_{d,s,t}
       \left(P^{\mathrm{dis}}_{d,c(s),t}-P^{\mathrm{ch}}_{d,c(s),t}\right)
     +\sum_h\kappa^{\mathrm{up}}_{d,s,h}r^{\mathrm{up}}_{d,h}
  \nonumber\\
   &+\sum_t\lambda^{\mathrm{act}}_{d,s,t}e^{\mathrm{del}}_{d,s,t}
     -\sum_t\lambda^{\mathrm{imb}}_{d,s,t}
       \left(e^{\mathrm{req}}_{d,s,t}-e^{\mathrm{del}}_{d,s,t}\right)
  \nonumber\\
   &-\sum_t\Delta t\lambda^{\mathrm{imb}}_{d,s,t}
       P^{\mathrm{rec}}_{d,s,t}.
  \label{eq:scenario-profit}
\end{align}
The five terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:scenario-profit} are day-ahead energy value,
capacity revenue, delivered-activation revenue, undelivered-activation cost,
and recovery-energy cost.  Every monetary term therefore depends on a physical
quantity produced by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:reserve-cap}--\eqref{eq:delivery-bound}.

For lower-tail profit risk, introduce a threshold $\zeta$ and nonnegative
shortfalls $\xi_s$.  The implemented lower-tail CVaR representation follows
Rockafellar and Uryasev~\cite{rockafellar2000}:
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{CVaR}^{\mathrm{lower}}_{\beta}(\Pi)
  &=\max_{\zeta,\xi}
  \left\{\zeta-\frac{1}{1-\beta}\sum_s\pi_s\xi_s\right\},
  \label{eq:cvar-expansion}\\
  \xi_s&\geq\zeta-\Pi_{d,s},
  \qquad \xi_s\geq0.
  \label{eq:cvar-shortfall}
\end{align}
With risk weight $\chi$ and $\beta=0.95$, the stochastic objective is
\begin{equation}
  \max_{x_d,\zeta,\xi}\quad
  (1-\chi)\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_d}\pi_s\Pi_{d,s}(x_d)
  +\chi\,\operatorname{CVaR}_{\beta}^{\mathrm{lower}}
       \!\left(\Pi_{d,s}(x_d)\right).
  \label{eq:risk-objective}
\end{equation}
where Eqs.~\eqref{eq:cvar-expansion}--\eqref{eq:cvar-shortfall} supply the
second term.  When $\chi=0$,
only expected profit remains; when $\chi>0$, low-profit scenarios affect the
solution through $\xi_s$.

The GRASCO sensitivity arms add a gate-visible charge-alignment quantity without
changing the market settlement ledger.  Let
$\omega_{d,c}=\sum_{s:c(s)=c}\pi_s$ be the probability mass assigned to
capacity-price cluster $c$.  For gate $g$, cluster $c$, and an hour in the
fixed midday set $\mathcal M_d$, define
\begin{align}
  0\leq q^{g}_{d,c,h}
  &\leq \widehat X^{\mathrm{proxy},g}_{d,h},
  \label{eq:forecast-overlap-proxy-bound}\\
  q^{g}_{d,c,h}
  &\leq \Delta t\sum_{t:h(t)=h}P^{\mathrm{ch}}_{d,c,t}.
  \label{eq:forecast-overlap-charge-bound}
\end{align}
Thus $q^{g}_{d,c,h}$ is the portion of planned hourly charging that can be
matched to the forecast proxy, rather than an assertion about the physical
source of the charged electricity.  Expected forecast-aligned charging is
\begin{equation}
  A^{g}_d(x_d)
  =\sum_c\omega_{d,c}\sum_{h\in\mathcal M_d}q^{g}_{d,c,h}.
  \label{eq:forecast-alignment-objective}
\end{equation}
Writing the market-only objective in Eq.~\eqref{eq:risk-objective} as
$J^{\mathrm{market}}_d(x_d)$, the exploratory family solves
\begin{equation}
  \max_{x_d,\zeta,\xi}\quad
  J^{\mathrm{market}}_d(x_d)+\rho A^g_d(x_d),
  \qquad \rho\in\{0,25,50,100\}\ \mathrm{EUR/MWh}.
  \label{eq:proxy-aware-objective}
\end{equation}
The coefficient $\rho$ is an experimental alignment credit, not observed
revenue.  The $\rho=0$ arm is the market-only control, and the positive values
form a sensitivity comparison rather than a selected social value.  CVaR is
still calculated from $\Pi_{d,s}$ alone, and all reported realized profit
excludes $\rho A^g_d$.

At 07:30 the common reserve is optimized with the configured delivery mode.
At 12:00 that reserve is fixed, the observed capacity price replaces its
scenario forecast, and the delivery-first implementation is lexicographic.  It
first obtains
\begin{equation}
  D_d^*=\max\sum_s\pi_s\sum_t e^{\mathrm{del}}_{d,s,t},
  \label{eq:delivery-first}
\end{equation}
and then solves Eq.~\eqref{eq:proxy-aware-objective} subject to expected delivery of
at least $D_d^*-\varepsilon_D$, where $\varepsilon_D=10^{-9}$ MWh.  At 07:30
Eq.~\eqref{eq:proxy-aware-objective} uses
$\widehat X^{\mathrm{proxy},07{:}30}$; at 12:00 the frozen reserve vector is
combined with $\widehat X^{\mathrm{proxy},12{:}00}$.  This
prevents the noon update from exchanging material delivery capability for a
small market or alignment improvement.  The output of this second solve is the deployed
vector in Eq.~\eqref{eq:decision-vector}; Eq.~\eqref{eq:delivery-first}
supplies its binding delivery floor, and realized market data then carry that
vector into the settlement trace below.

\section{Fifteen-minute settlement trace and linkage diagnostics}

For each deployed strategy, realized activation first converts the frozen
reserve decision into an observed request:
\begin{equation}
  e^{\mathrm{req,obs}}_{d,t}
  =r^{\mathrm{up}}_{d,h(t)}a^{\mathrm{obs}}_{d,t}.
  \label{eq:realized-required-activation}
\end{equation}
Holding $u_d$ fixed, the settlement closure maximizes total delivered
activation subject to Eqs.~\eqref{eq:realized-soc}--\eqref{eq:delivery-bound}
with realized inputs, then minimizes the imbalance-price cost of
$P^{\mathrm{rec}}$.  This closure produces
$e^{\mathrm{del}}$, $P^{\mathrm{rec}}$, and $S^{\mathrm{real}}$; it does not
change charge, discharge, or reserve.  The ex-post trace records
\begin{equation}
  z_{d,t}=\left(
  P^{\mathrm{ch}}_t,P^{\mathrm{dis}}_t,r^{\mathrm{up}}_{h(t)},
  e^{\mathrm{req}}_t,e^{\mathrm{del}}_t,
  P^{\mathrm{rec}}_t,S^{\mathrm{plan}}_t,S^{\mathrm{real}}_t,
  \Pi^{\mathrm{energy}}_t,\Pi^{\mathrm{cap}}_t,
  \Pi^{\mathrm{act}}_t,C^{\mathrm{short}}_t,C^{\mathrm{rec}}_t
  \right).
  \label{eq:trace-vector}
\end{equation}
Let $n_{d,h}=|\{t\in\mathcal T_d:h(t)=h\}|$.  The trace components are linked
to the physical variables by
\begin{align}
  \Pi^{\mathrm{energy}}_{d,t}
  &=\Delta t\lambda^{\mathrm{DA}}_{d,t}
  \left(P^{\mathrm{dis}}_{d,t}-P^{\mathrm{ch}}_{d,t}\right),
  \label{eq:trace-energy}\\
  \Pi^{\mathrm{cap}}_{d,t}
  &=\frac{\kappa^{\mathrm{up}}_{d,h(t)}r^{\mathrm{up}}_{d,h(t)}}
  {n_{d,h(t)}},
  \label{eq:trace-capacity}\\
  \Pi^{\mathrm{act}}_{d,t}
  &=\lambda^{\mathrm{act}}_{d,t}e^{\mathrm{del}}_{d,t},
  \label{eq:trace-activation}\\
  C^{\mathrm{short}}_{d,t}
  &=\lambda^{\mathrm{imb}}_{d,t}
  \left(e^{\mathrm{req,obs}}_{d,t}-e^{\mathrm{del}}_{d,t}\right),
  \label{eq:trace-shortfall}\\
  C^{\mathrm{rec}}_{d,t}
  &=\Delta t\lambda^{\mathrm{imb}}_{d,t}P^{\mathrm{rec}}_{d,t}.
  \label{eq:trace-recovery}
\end{align}
The variables listed in Eq.~\eqref{eq:trace-vector} are therefore either
frozen physical decisions, realized closure outputs, or the five monetary
components in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:trace-energy}--\eqref{eq:trace-recovery}.
Hourly capacity revenue is therefore allocated equally across its observed
15-minute slots.  Summing
$\Pi^{\mathrm{energy}}+\Pi^{\mathrm{cap}}+\Pi^{\mathrm{act}}
-C^{\mathrm{short}}-C^{\mathrm{rec}}$ over $t$ reproduces the daily settlement
profit $\Pi_d$.  This equality is the trace-to-daily audit used below.

The daily diagnostic vector is
\begin{equation}
  J_d=\left(
  E^{\mathrm{mid}}_{\mathrm{ch},d},
  A^{12{:}00}_d,
  E^{\mathrm{overlap}}_{\mathrm{ch},d},
  AS^{\mathrm{ch}}_d,AR^{\mathrm{proxy}}_d,
  S_{d,17},R^{\mathrm{eve}}_d,
  E^{\mathrm{req,eve}}_d,E^{\mathrm{del,eve}}_d,
  U^{\mathrm{eve}}_d,C^{\mathrm{rec}}_d,\Pi_d
  \right),
  \label{eq:diagnostic-vector}
\end{equation}
The first two entries record midday charge and its overlap with the 12:00
forecast; the next three couple the ex-post proxy branch to deployed charge;
$S_{d,17}$ is read from the realized SOC trace; the next four entries aggregate
the frozen reserve and realized activation over $\mathcal E_d$; and the final
two entries aggregate the monetary trace.  Thus $J_d$ is downstream of both
$X^{\mathrm{proxy}}$ and $z_{d,t}$, as shown in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:research-chain} and~\eqref{eq:proxy-diagnostic-chain}.
where
\begin{align}
  R^{\mathrm{eve}}_d
  &=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{E}_d}r^{\mathrm{up}}_{h(t)}\Delta t,
  \label{eq:evening-reserve}\\
  E^{\mathrm{req,eve}}_d
  &=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{E}_d}e^{\mathrm{req}}_t,
  \qquad
  E^{\mathrm{del,eve}}_d
  =\sum_{t\in\mathcal{E}_d}e^{\mathrm{del}}_t,
  \label{eq:evening-activation}\\
  U^{\mathrm{eve}}_d
  &=E^{\mathrm{req,eve}}_d-E^{\mathrm{del,eve}}_d,
  \qquad
  DR^{\mathrm{eve}}
  =\frac{\sum_dE^{\mathrm{del,eve}}_d}
  {\sum_dE^{\mathrm{req,eve}}_d},
  \label{eq:evening-delivery}\\
  C^{\mathrm{rec}}_d
  &=\sum_t\Delta t\lambda^{\mathrm{imb}}_tP^{\mathrm{rec}}_t.
  \label{eq:recovery-cost}
\end{align}
Equations~\eqref{eq:evening-reserve}--\eqref{eq:evening-delivery} aggregate
the hourly reserve and interval activation entries of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:diagnostic-vector}; Eq.~\eqref{eq:recovery-cost} is the daily
sum of Eq.~\eqref{eq:trace-recovery}.
The observable linkage is summarized by
\begin{align}
  \Gamma_{S}
  &=\operatorname{Corr}\!\left(E^{\mathrm{mid}}_{\mathrm{ch},d},S_{d,17}\right),
  \label{eq:soc-link}\\
  \Gamma_{R}
  &=\operatorname{Corr}\!\left(E^{\mathrm{mid}}_{\mathrm{ch},d},R^{\mathrm{eve}}_d\right).
  \label{eq:reserve-link}
\end{align}
Equations~\eqref{eq:soc-link} and~\eqref{eq:reserve-link} reuse the same
upstream variable $E^{\mathrm{mid}}_{\mathrm{ch},d}$ but connect it to two
different downstream outcomes: stored energy and reserve commitment.
A physically useful midday--evening link requires $\Gamma_S>0$.  A decision
protocol that actively converts midday charging into additional evening reserve
would also be expected to have $\Gamma_R\geq0$, unless the reserve decision is
already power-saturated or frozen before the midday information becomes
available.

a point forecast, and SO denotes stochastic optimization driven by scenarios.

DD-CC, MPF, and W\&SPP are reconstructed under the same DK1 data, battery,

distribution and chance-constraint idea of Toubeau et al.~\cite{toubeau2021},

frozen DK1 hourly quantile approximation.  MPF transfers the sequential
scenario-update structure of Al-Lawati et al.~\cite{allawati2021}, but reverses
the original day-ahead-to-reserve order to the DK1 reserve-to-day-ahead order,
uses 50 rather than 90 scenarios, and removes the wind farm, intraday market,
and down reserve.  W\&SPP transfers the three-stage scenario-tree and
expectation--CVaR structure of Mancini et al.~\cite{mancini2024}, but replaces
the German four-hour product and 1.5-hour reserve duration by hourly DK1 aFRR
and the common one-hour assumption, removes the co-located wind farm and
binaries, and uses task-specified scenario counts and risk levels.  These
differences preclude claims of numerical replication; the comparison tests the
published decision ideas under one controlled DK1 protocol.

therefore the value of probabilistic scenarios over point dispatch, not the
universal superiority of one copula.

\subsection{Diagnosis of the observed problem}

On the 206 matched decision days, the midday proxy contains 318,957 MWh.  Among
the nine market-only strategies, the largest overlap is 449.5 MWh for the
LGBM-DO.
Their maximum observed proxy-absorption ratio is
\begin{equation}
  \max_m AR^{\mathrm{proxy}}_m
  =\frac{449.5}{318{,}957}=0.001409=0.1409\%.
  \label{eq:maximum-proxy-absorption}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:maximum-proxy-absorption} is the full-sample aggregation of
the daily numerator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:overlap-charge} divided by the matched-day
proxy-energy denominator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:proxy-absorption-ratio}; it is not an
average of daily percentages.
For the scenario-based and published-method market-only reconstructions, the
corresponding range is 0.0116--0.0698\%.  Their realized alignment share is
34--44\%.  GRASCO-$25$ through GRASCO-$100$ raise that share to 48.9--56.4\%,
and GRASCO-$100$ reaches
\begin{equation}
  AR^{\mathrm{proxy}}_{\rho=100}
  =\frac{608.0}{318{,}957}=0.001906=0.1906\%.
  \label{eq:proxy-aware-maximum-absorption}
\end{equation}
Comparing Eq.~\eqref{eq:proxy-aware-maximum-absorption} with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:maximum-proxy-absorption} shows that the implemented signal
changes timing in the intended direction, but even the largest sensitivity
remains below 0.2\% of midday proxy energy.

The observed linkage statistics are
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_S\in[0.33,0.52],
  \qquad
  \Gamma_R\in[-0.52,-0.28],
  \qquad
  DR^{\mathrm{eve}}\in[97.38\%,98.56\%].
  \label{eq:observed-linkage}
\end{equation}
The three ranges in Eq.~\eqref{eq:observed-linkage} come directly from
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:soc-link}, \eqref{eq:reserve-link}, and
\eqref{eq:evening-delivery}, respectively.
The correlations are descriptive rather than causal.  Their joint pattern is
nevertheless informative: midday charging consistently raises the 17:00 SOC,
but days with more midday charging do not receive more evening reserve.  This
is consistent with the frozen gate sequence and the near-saturation of reserve
power, rather than with a failure of the battery to carry energy from midday to
the evening.

Across the full day, 74--88\% of undelivered activation is attributable to
gross-power headroom, while only 12--26\% is attributable to the SOC-energy
envelope.  Evening shortfall averages only 0.021--0.040 MWh/day.  Thus the
remaining delivery problem is mainly simultaneous use of battery power by the
energy schedule and activation, not inadequate evening SOC.

\section{Research claim and remaining uncertainty}

The empirical claim of the present DK1 study is:
\begin{quote}
For a price-taking 4 MW/8 MWh battery participating in day-ahead energy and
upward aFRR, probabilistic scheduling improves reserve value, activation
delivery, and recovery timing, but the market-only schedules produce little
charging aligned with the DK1 VRE-above-load proxy.  Supplying the gate-safe
WLPF signal to the proposed GRASCO alignment objective increases forecast and
realized overlap, midday charging, and 17:00 SOC monotonically with $\rho$.
It does not materially change evening reserve or delivered activation because
reserve is fixed at 07:30 and already close to the battery power limit.  The
profit response is non-monotone: GRASCO-$25$ improves mean realized profit on
this sample, GRASCO-$50$ is statistically inconclusive, and GRASCO-$100$ lowers
profit.  These proxy-positive observations represent renewable-attributable
net-export pressure rather than measured curtailment.
\end{quote}

This claim is narrower than a claim of actual curtailment reduction.  It is
useful to state the decision chain implemented only by the $\rho>0$ arms:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat X^{\mathrm{proxy},07{:}30}_{d,h}
  \longrightarrow r^{\mathrm{up}}_{d,h},P^{\mathrm{ch}}_{d,c,t}
  \longrightarrow \widehat X^{\mathrm{proxy},12{:}00}_{d,h}
  \longrightarrow P^{\mathrm{ch}}_t
  \longrightarrow S_{17}
  \longrightarrow e^{\mathrm{del}}_{17{:}21}
  \longrightarrow C^{\mathrm{rec}},
  \label{eq:proxy-decision-chain}
\end{equation}
GRASCO-$0$ sets $\rho=0$, so WLPF has no objective value; the three positive
sensitivity arms implement Eq.~\eqref{eq:proxy-decision-chain} through
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:forecast-overlap-proxy-bound}--\eqref{eq:proxy-aware-objective}.
The realized proxy $X^{\mathrm{proxy}}$ still enters only ex-post diagnostics.
The remaining uncertainty is not whether the signal can change charge timing,
which the experiment establishes, but how the economic trade-off varies with
forecast quality, valuation, battery scale, and unobserved system curtailment.

The numerical evidence is stored in the proxy-surplus, decomposition, and
gate-forecast tables, the daily and summary midday--evening tables, and the nine
market-only and three GRASCO renewable-alignment dispatch traces.
The experiment result manifest records the exact output names.  The
trace-to-daily aggregation error is at most $5.14\times10^{-8}$, confirming
that the new ledger reproduces rather than changes the frozen settlement
results.

\section{Aggregation rules and admissible claims}

Metrics are evaluated only on common intervals for which all required variables are observed. Missing values are not replaced by zeros, interpolated, or extrapolated. For multi-day or full-sample results, ratios are computed as the sum of their numerators divided by the sum of their denominators; daily ratios are not averaged without weights. If the denominator of Eq.~\eqref{eq:alignment-share} or Eq.~\eqref{eq:proxy-absorption-ratio} is zero, the corresponding daily metric is recorded as missing rather than zero.

The primary terminology for the present study is ``charging aligned with renewable-rich midday periods,'' ``potential renewable absorption,'' and ``midday-to-evening energy shifting.'' The terms ``actual renewable utilization rate'' and ``actual curtailment reduction'' require unconstrained available VRE, observed curtailment or dispatch-down information, and a system-level counterfactual market-clearing model.

\section{Relation to established formulations}

Equations~\eqref{eq:true-curtailment-power}--\eqref{eq:true-utilization-rate} follow the common system-dispatch distinction between available VRE and utilized VRE. The National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL) represents curtailment as the unused portion of otherwise available variable generation and evaluates storage within the system balance~\cite{nrel2016storage}. Its formulation for DC-coupled PV--battery systems further tracks curtailment recovered by storage charging within an explicit energy balance~\cite{nrel2021pvb}. Peng et al. jointly model generation, curtailment, transmission, and storage operation in SWITCH-China and constrain renewable-connected batteries to charge from co-located renewable generation~\cite{peng2023heterogeneous}. These studies can evaluate system curtailment or renewable charging provenance because their models contain system balances, available generation, or source-coupling constraints that are absent from the present price-taking DK1 simulation.
